Windward Theme Reference

CONCEPT

  A maritime / open-ocean aesthetic built around the colors of a tall
  ship cutting through deep water under a bright sky — extended into a
  full cohesive palette.

  The feeling: standing at the rail of a clipper ship mid-voyage — deep
  teal-black water below, sun-bleached canvas sails overhead, a wide pale
  sky, brass fittings gone warm with salt air, and the rust-red warning
  of a hull that's seen weather. Foam breaks white against the bow.
  Distant coastline fades to olive-grey on the horizon. A world in
  motion, always heading somewhere.

EXTENDING THE THEME TO NEW APPS

  When theming a new application, follow this decision tree:

  Background?       → abyss #0a1a1d or deep #123338
  Surface/card?     → deep #123338 or shadow #1e4b4f
  Text?             → canvas #ede3c8 (primary)  mist #7fa89f (secondary)
  Accent/highlight? → teal #1f7a6c (strong)  foam #4fa88f (soft)
  Bright accent?    → aqua #5fd9c4
  Border?           → teal #1f7a6c (active)  shadow #1e4b4f (inactive)
  Hover?            → dusk #2d6b66
  Success?          → kelp #6fbf73
  Warning?          → brass #c9a24a
  Error?            → rust #c9603f
  Info?             → cyan #a8d8e0
  Shadow/glow?      → rgba(1f7a6c, 0.25–0.40)

  Readability note (added after the Obsidian theme build):
  raw mist/dusk/shadow/teal/abyss read beautifully on a terminal
  bar or status line where the surrounding chrome is small and the
  eye isn't lingering, but they get muddy fast — or in abyss's case,
  tiring to read against — as body text, long-form borders, or any
  surface read continuously for more than a second (reading apps,
  editors, docs). For those contexts, swap in the derived tones
  instead:
    main reading surface   → parchment #14282a    (not raw abyss)
    secondary/muted text   → mist-text #9dbdb5    (not raw mist)
    inactive borders/hr    → border-soft #2a4f4f  (not raw shadow)
    visible/hover borders  → border-strong #3a6863 (not raw dusk)
    accent needing to read
      at text size (links,
      headings, focus rings) → teal-accent #2a9683 or
                                 link-heading #4fd0b8 (not raw teal)
  Rule of thumb: if it's a UI accent glanced at (status icon, bar
  segment) use the raw palette; if it's read continuously (body
  text, link color, a border you have to find, the page you write
  on) use the lifted/desaturated tone.

  Rule: pure black (#000000) and pure white (#ffffff) are never used.
  The darkest point is abyss #0a1a1d, lightest is canvas #ede3c8.
  Every color has a teal/sea undertone — even the golds and reds lean
  slightly cool/green rather than warm/orange.
